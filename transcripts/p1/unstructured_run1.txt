## Practical Context
An orbital period of roughly 95.5 minutes (or about 1.59" hours" ) is a classic characteristic of Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites. For example, a 550" km" altitude is famously utilized by SpaceX's Starlink constellation because it provides a good balance: it is high enough to avoid rapid orbital decay from atmospheric drag, yet low enough to ensure low latency and strong signal transmission back to Earth.
