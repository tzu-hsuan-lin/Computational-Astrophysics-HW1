import numpy as np

G = 6.67430e-11 # m^3 kg^-1 s^-2

R_E = 6371.0 * 1000 # meters (mean Earth radius)

r = R_E + h
T_seconds = 2 * np.pi * np.sqrt(r**3 / mu)
T_minutes = T_seconds / 60

print(f"Period (minutes): {T_minutes}")

To find the orbital period of a satellite in a circular orbit at an altitude of 550 km, we start from Newton's law of universal gravitation and Newton's second law of motion.


1. Derivation of the Orbital Period
For a satellite of mass m orbiting a planet of mass M (Earth) at a distance r from the center of the planet, the only force acting on the satellite in a circular orbit is the gravitational pull.

Equating the gravitational force to the centripetal force:

GMm/r^2 =(mv^2)/r
Where:


	G is the gravitational constant

	M is the mass of the Earth

	m is the mass of the satellite

	r is the orbital radius from the center of Earth (r=R_E+h)

	v is the orbital speed

Canceling the satellite mass m and solving for the orbital velocity v:

v=√(GM/r)
The orbital period T is the time required to complete one full circumference (2πr) at speed v:

T=2πr/v=2πr/√(GM/r)
Simplifying this expression yields Kepler's Third Law for circular orbits:

T=2π√(r^3/GM)

2. Parameter Substitution & Evaluation
Using standard Earth parameters:


	Mean Earth radius (R_E): 6,371" km"=6.371×10^6 " m" 

	Altitude (h): 550" km"=5.5×10^5 " m" 

	Standard gravitational parameter (μ=GM): 3.986×10^14 〖" m" 〗^3/"s" ^2

First, calculate the total orbital radius r:

r=R_E+h=6,371" km"+550" km"=6,921" km"=6.921×10^6 " m" 
Next, substitute r and μ into the period formula:

T=2π√((6.921×10^6 " m" )^3/(3.986×10^14 〖" m" 〗^3/"s" ^2 ))
T≈5,730.1" seconds" 
Converting seconds into minutes:

T_"minutes" =5,730.1" s" /60" s/min" ≈95.5" minutes" 
(Note: If using the WGS84 equatorial radius of 6,378.1" km" , the orbital period evaluates to approximately 95.6 minutes).
